Loading metre calculator: work out loading metres (ldm)

A loading metre (ldm) is one metre of load floor across the full width of 2.40 m. The formula is length × width ÷ 2.4 (in metres): a euro pallet takes 0.4 ldm, an industrial pallet 0.5 ldm. A curtainsider has around 13.6 ldm – room for 34 euro pallets in a single tier.

Stacked goods take no extra floor space – enter the number of tiers for that. The chargeable weight appears once you enter your forwarder’s rate: there is no universal one.

How loading metres are calculated

Loading metres measure floor area, not volume: how much length the goods take up on a 2.40 m wide load floor. That is why height does not appear in the formula.

Loading metres = length (m) × width (m) ÷ 2.4
  • Euro pallet 1.20 × 0.80 m: 0.96 m² ÷ 2.4 = 0.4 ldm. 20 of them take 8.0 ldm.
  • Industrial pallet 1.20 × 1.00 m: 1.2 m² ÷ 2.4 = 0.5 ldm.
  • Crate 3.00 × 1.50 m: 4.5 m² ÷ 2.4 = 1.875 ldm.

The 3D calculator also shows the occupied load length: from the headboard to the rearmost pallet. It equals the formula when the goods stand without gaps – and is larger when gaps remain (two industrial pallets side by side leave 48 cm free) or when the calculator spreads pallets towards the rear for the axle load. Which of the two is billed is set out in your forwarder’s terms.

How many pallets fit on a truck?

Dividing the curtainsider’s 13.6 ldm by the loading metres per pallet gives you a number. Whether it holds depends on whether the pallets can be laid across the width without gaps:

For euro pallets the formula works out: three across are exactly 2.40 m. For industrial pallets it does not – two side by side are 2.00 m and the rest of the width stays empty, hence 26 instead of 27. Wire mesh pallets go the other way and fit one more than the formula, because the calculator works with a trailer 2.48 m wide inside and turns the boxes in a mixed pattern. The right-hand column comes from the 3D calculator, which actually places the pallets; the formula is the billing figure.

Stacked: fewer loading metres

What stands on top of something takes no extra floor. 26 euro pallets in two tiers need 13 floor positions and so 5.2 instead of 10.4 ldm. In the calculator above, enter the number of tiers for that. Whether your forwarder bills stacked goods by loading metres or charges extra for non-stackable goods is set out in their terms.

Loading metres and weight: the chargeable weight

For part and groupage loads, many forwarders charge the higher of two figures: the real weight and loading metres × a fixed rate in kg. There is no universal rate – each forwarder sets it in their terms. Enter it above and the calculator works out the chargeable weight.

Example with an assumed rate of 1,750 kg per ldm: 10 euro pallets at 300 kg take 4.0 ldm. 4.0 × 1,750 = 7,000 kg – more than the real 3,000 kg, so 7,000 kg are charged. If the same pallets weigh 900 kg each, the real 9,000 kg count.

Loading metres, floor positions, cubic metres – which for what?

  • Loading metres measure floor area on the truck – the usual unit for part loads by road.
  • Floor positions count how many pallets fit on the floor – see floor positions in a container.
  • Cubic metres measure volume – the unit of sea freight (LCL), see how to calculate CBM.

Frequent questions

What is a loading metre?
One metre of load floor across the full width of 2.40 m. The formula is length × width ÷ 2.4 in metres.

How many loading metres does a truck have?
A curtainsider has around 13.6 ldm, a swap body around 7.5 and a rigid truck around 7.2.

How many euro pallets fit on a truck?
On a curtainsider 34 in a single tier, more when stacked, as long as height and payload allow.

How do you convert loading metres into weight?
With your forwarder’s rate: the chargeable weight is the higher of the real weight and loading metres × rate. There is no statutory or universal rate.
