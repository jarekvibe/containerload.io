How many pallets fit in a 20ft container?

A 20ft standard container holds 9 standard US pallets (48 × 40 in, GMA) floor-loaded in one tier — not the 10 you will read elsewhere. Double-stacked with low cargo: 18.

Why not 10? Check the math

Two rows of five sounds plausible — until you measure. Five pallets of 48 in lengthwise need 240 in of length; the interior is about 232 in (590 cm). Turn them instead, and two 48-in pallets side by side need 96 in of width; the container offers 92.5 in (235 cm). Either way the tenth pallet exists only on paper. Floor-loaded without overhang, 9 is the honest number.

How the 9 are placed — calculated, not copied

Stacked: two tiers with low cargo

If each loaded pallet stays below about 42 in (107 cm), stackable cargo fits in two tiers: 18 pallets (interior height 94 in / 239 cm). A 20ft high cube (106 in / 270 cm) allows the second tier up to about 48 in per loaded pallet. Weight is rarely the limit here: 18 pallets at 1,100 lbs are under 20,000 lbs of roughly 62,000 lbs container payload — on the road, the truck limits bite first.
