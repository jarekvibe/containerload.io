How many 1100 × 1100 pallets fit in a 20ft and a 40ft container?

A 20ft container takes 10 pallets of 1100 × 1100 mm floor-loaded in one tier, a 40ft container 20. Double-stacked with low cargo: 20 and 40. Shipping euro pallets or 48 × 40 in pallets instead? Those grids work differently.

In short

The 1100 × 1100 mm pallet is the standard footprint in Japan and South Korea — there it is called T11 — and common across Asia; it is one of the six pallet sizes listed in ISO 6780. It was sized for the ISO container: two side by side take 220 of the 235 cm interior width, leaving just 15 cm. Nothing to turn, nothing to puzzle out — the count is simply rows of two.

How the 10 are placed — calculated, not copied

A 20ft container is 590 cm long inside. Five pallets in a row take 550 cm, so there are five rows of two = 10, with 40 cm left at the door. Because the footprint is square, turning a pallet changes nothing: with or without rotation the packer finds the same 10. This is the layout from above:

Why a 40ft takes 20 — and not 21 or 22

A 40ft container is 1,203 cm long inside. Ten rows of two take 1,100 cm; an eleventh row would need 1,210 cm — 7 cm more than the container has. So the count stops at 20, with 103 cm of floor left at the door for loose cargo or securing. A 45ft high cube (1,355 cm) fits twelve rows: 24 pallets.

More floor than euro pallets — in a 20ft

Ten pallets of 1100 × 1100 cover 12.1 m² of a 20ft floor. The best euro pallet stow is 11 pallets, which cover 10.6 m² — the square pallet uses the short container noticeably better. In a 40ft the two are nearly level: 20 × 1100 × 1100 cover 24.2 m², 25 euro pallets 24.0 m².

Stacked: two tiers with low cargo

If each loaded pallet stays below about 110 cm, stackable cargo fits in two tiers: 20 pallets in a 20ft and 40 in a 40ft standard container (interior height 239 cm). A high cube (270 cm) allows two tiers up to about 125 cm per loaded pallet — same floor, same positions.

Weight: two tiers in a 40ft are limited by payload

A 40ft standard container takes about 26,600 kg of payload. In one tier that is up to 1,330 kg per pallet — rarely the limit. In two tiers the 40 pallets may average only 665 kg each before the container is full by weight, not by space. A 20ft (about 28,200 kg) leaves 1,410 kg per pallet even double-stacked. Road limits are often lower than the container's own — see the road weight limits guide.
