Open top container: dimensions, roof opening, loading

The open top is the container for crane loading from above and for over-height cargo. Its most important dimension is not the inside — it is the roof opening, and that is smaller than many plan for.

The dimensions at a glance

All values in centimetres, as reference values — open tops vary by a few centimetres between manufacturers. They are the same values the 3D calculator uses for its loading-path checks.

The roof opening is smaller than the inside

Side rails and the header above the door take away length and width: on the 20ft, about 49 cm of length and 13 cm of width compared to the inside. In practice that means a piece can fit the container on paper and still be unloadable — it passes neither through the roof nor through the door. The 3D calculator checks exactly these two loading paths on an open top and only flags a conflict when both fail.

Over-height: what the open top is for

Cargo may stick out over the open roof. It then counts against the ISO envelope of the slot (height 259 cm): anything above blocks the slot overhead on board — technically fine, but freight-relevant. The calculator shows over-height and blocked slots in its OOG card before the booking goes out.

Related questions

Open top or flat rack?
Open top when the cargo fits the container in length and width and only height or the loading path (crane) is the issue. For over-width or over-length the open roof does not help — that is flat rack territory.

What about the tarpaulin?
Standard OTs run with roof bows and a tarp. For stowage planning that changes nothing about the opening sizes — the bows come out for loading.
