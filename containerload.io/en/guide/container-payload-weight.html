Container Guide · Payload & weight

How much payload does a container have?

A 20ft container takes around 28 tonnes of payload, a 40ft container around 26.6 tonnes. Surprisingly, the smaller 20ft often has the higher permitted payload — with heavy goods it is weight, not size, that is the real limit.

Max. payload at a glance

The permitted payload is the gross weight minus the container's tare weight. What counts is always the CSC plate on the container itself; the following are common guide values:

ContainerVolumeMax. payload
20′ Standard≈ 33 m³≈ 28.2 t
20′ High Cube≈ 37 m³≈ 28.1 t
40′ Standard≈ 68 m³≈ 26.6 t
40′ High Cube≈ 76 m³≈ 26.6 t
45′ High Cube≈ 86 m³≈ 27.6 t

Weight or volume — which runs out first?

Heavy, dense goods (tiles, beverages, metal) usually reach the weight limit long before the container is full. Light, bulky goods hit the volume first. The 3D calculator shows both in parallel and warns when your cargo exceeds the payload — even when there would still be space.
