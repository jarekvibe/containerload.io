How to use the map

Drag to pan, use the mouse wheel or two fingers to zoom, and click a dot to see the port details. The search finds every port by name, by code (e.g. USLGB for Long Beach) or by country. Once you pick a country, its coasts appear below — for the USA the West Coast, East Coast and Gulf Coast, for Germany the North Sea and the Baltic.

Without a search term the map shows the large and medium ports; "Also small ports" adds the rest. Every selection is stored in the address bar — share the link and the recipient sees the same.

Where the data comes from

Seaports: the World Port Index (Pub. 150) of the US National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency, April 2025 edition — public domain. Because the agency's site was unreachable when the map was built, the file comes from the compilation seaport-data-js by Aashish Vivekanand (CC BY 4.0); we use name, code, location, region, water body, basin size, depths and facilities.

Container ports: the World Shipping Council's top 50 list with 2025 throughput. Only these 50 are called "container port" on the map — for a reason: in the World Port Index the container-facilities field is empty precisely for the large ports (Hamburg, Rotterdam and Los Angeles read "not recorded"), and harbour size means the basin, not the throughput (Felixstowe, the UK's largest container port, is "very small" there). Which of the other ports container lines call at is not shown yet; check with your forwarder or the carrier.
