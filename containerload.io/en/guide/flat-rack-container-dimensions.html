Flat rack container: dimensions, payload, out of gauge

The flat rack is the heavy lifter among containers: open deck, end walls, and with about 39 t payload on the 40ft far more capacity than any standard box. Its territory: over-width, over-length, heavy lifts.

The dimensions at a glance

All values in centimetres, as reference values — flat racks vary considerably by type (fixed or collapsible end walls). They are the values the 3D calculator plans with.

The platform (20′: deck 606 × 244 cm, 40′: 1219 × 244 cm, payload up to about 40 t) is the flat rack without end walls — a bare deck for project cargo.

Out of gauge: flat rack territory

Cargo may overhang the deck to the sides, the front and the top. It is measured against the ISO envelope of the slot: anything beyond it blocks neighbouring slots on board and costs freight accordingly. The 3D calculator measures over-width, over-length and over-height to the centimetre and shows the blocked slots in its OOG card — with centred stowage on request, the way project cargo wants it.

More capacity than any standard container

About 31 t (20′) and 39 t (40′) versus roughly 26 to 28 t for a GP: flat racks are built for concentrated loads. For point loads (machine feet, transformers) load spreading and timber bedding still belong in the carrier discussion — payload alone does not say the deck carries the weight at that exact spot.
